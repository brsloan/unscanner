        Smaller files, in both formats. Grayscale drops the colour from every figure: not for a document whose figures rely on it.
          JPEG suits photographs and halftones and is much smaller; line drawings and charts stay sharper as PNG.
      
      Indent-style: no space between paragraphs and a first-line indent, as in a printed book (none after a heading or a break).
        Justified text is harder to read for some people. Without page numbers the printed pages can no longer be cited or navigated;
        words the page breaks fell in are joined up. Images inside the file make the HTML one file that works on its own
        (open it with a double-click, or upload it to Brightspace) and about a third larger; without it, keep the figures folder next to it.
        Bookmarks put a collapsed list of the document's headings at the top of the HTML, each one a link, with branches that fold away;
        an EPUB always has this as its own table of contents.
        Build again to apply.

    Changes apply to the next page transcribed; pages already done keep their text until they are transcribed again.
      They are saved as text files in the work folder's prompts folder, so they travel with your work and survive updates.
      The output format the program reads is fixed and not shown here. An empty box means the built-in text.
    
      Who is doing the work and why
      Sent first, with every page and every +Table. It keeps faithful transcription from being mistaken for a
        copyright problem, so make every statement in it true for your institution.

      What Unscanner does
      
      
      A scanned PDF is really a set of photographs of pages. A screen reader cannot read a photograph aloud, text cannot be
        made larger or searched, and the headings cannot be used to jump around. Unscanner turns the scan into real text that
        everyone can use.
      
      
      An AI model reads each page and writes out its text. You check that text against the scan, correct anything it got
        wrong, and approve the page. When every page is done, Unscanner makes two files: a web page (HTML) and an e-book (EPUB).
        Both keep the original printed page numbers, so a reader can still cite and find “page 42”.
      
      
      Your original PDF is never changed.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      The steps, start to finish
      
      
        
        
      1. Open PDF… and choose the scan. The first time you open a PDF, a box asks for its title, author and
          language; the title and author are often printed on the first pages, and you can look at the scan while you type.
        2. 
        
      2. Transcribe… sends the pages to the AI model, which writes out their text. This runs on its own; you
          can start checking pages that are finished while the rest are still being read.
        3. 
        
      3. Go through the pages one at a time. Compare the text on the right with the scan in the middle, correct what is wrong,
          and click Approve & next (or press F2).
        4. 
        
      4. Build makes the finished web page and e-book. HTML and EPUB buttons
          then appear in the top bar to download them.
        5. 
        
      5. Validate checks the result for accessibility problems. Click a problem to go to its page, fix it,
          and Build again.
        6. 
      
      
      
      You can stop at any point and come back later: open the project again from File > Recent projects.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Finding your way around
      
      
      The window has three columns:
      
      
        
        
      • On the left, the list of pages, or of the document’s headings.
        • 
        
      • In the middle, the scanned page, as a picture.
        • 
        
      • On the right, the text of that page, which you can edit like a word processor document.
        • 
      
      
      
      Drag the thin bars between the columns to make one wider. The line at the very bottom of the window shows what just
        happened, such as “Saved” or an error; click ▲ at its right end to read a long message in full.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      The list of pages
      
      
      Click a page to show it. The coloured dot beside each page says how far along it is:
      
      
        
        
      •  Grey: not read yet.
        • 
        
      •  Amber: read by the model, or saved by you, but not
          approved yet.
        • 
        
      •  Green: approved by a person.
        • 
        
      •  Red: something went wrong reading it; hover over the page to see
          why, then try Transcribe again for that page or type the text yourself.
        • 
      
      
      
      The number on the right of a page is its printed page number. A pencil (✎) means it has changes you have not saved.
        A small M means the AI model wrote it last, and a C that Claude, working as an assistant
        alongside you, changed it.
      
      
      Show picks which pages are listed: all of them, only those that still need work, or only those with
        pictures or tables. The ◀ ▶ arrows and Approve & next then step through just those pages.
      
      
      To change several pages at once, hold Ctrl and click each one (or click one and Shift+click another to pick all the
        pages between), then right-click for Approve, Needs review, Skip or Don’t skip.
      
      
      The Headings tab lists the document’s headings, like the navigation pane in Word. Click one to go to
        it. Click the small triangle beside a heading to fold away or show the headings under it.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      The scanned page
      
      
        
        
      • ◀ ▶ go to the previous and next page.
        • 
        
      • Fit shows the whole page; Fill makes it as wide as the column. The slider on the
          right zooms in and out.
        • 
        
      • Click a word on the scan to put the cursor on that word in the text.
        • 
        
      • Follow scrolls the scan to keep up with where you are typing, and Mark puts a red
          dot on the scan beside that word, so you never lose your place.
        • 
        
      • Diff (in the top bar) highlights the differences between the scan and the text. Words that are on the
          scan but missing from the text get a red box on the scan; words that differ get a dashed amber box. In the text, extra
          or different words are underlined the same way. The number on the button counts the differences. It is a guide, not a
          verdict: running heads and page numbers are meant to be left out, and they show up as differences too.
        • 
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Correcting the text
      
      
      Type in the text column as you would in any word processor. The toolbar above it:
      
      
      
        
      Paragraph / Heading 1–6 / …
      
        
      Shows what kind of text the cursor is in. Choose another to change it. Headings matter: screen reader users jump
          from heading to heading, so use Heading 1 for the title only, Heading 2 for chapters, Heading 3 for sections within
          them, and so on, without skipping a level.
      
        
      B and I
      
        
      Bold and italic, where the printed page uses them for emphasis.
      
        
      x2
      
        
      Raised text, as in footnote numbers.
      
        
      Aa
      
        
      Small capitals, as printed. Type the words normally; Unscanner shows them in small capitals.
      
        
      • and 1.
      
        
      Bulleted and numbered lists. n. sets the number a list starts at, for a list that carries on from
          the page before.
      
        
      Alignment buttons
      
        
      Left, centred or right, for headings and lines that are set that way on the page.
      
        
      Alt, +Fig
      
        
      For pictures; see Pictures.
      
        
      +Table
      
        
      See Tables.
      
        
      Ω
      
        
      Inserts a symbol that is hard to type, such as ½, —, § or °.
      
        
      Lang
      
        
      Marks the selected words as another language (for example fr for French or la for Latin),
          so a screen reader pronounces them correctly.
      
        
      Clear
      
        
      Removes bold, italic and other formatting from the selected text.
      
        
      Source
      
        
      Shows the page as HTML code. Only needed for unusual fixes; you can ignore it.
      
        
      Draft
      
        
      Shows the rough text the computer found on the scan before the AI model read it. Handy for copying a passage the
          model skipped.
      
      
      
      
      Only formatting that helps a reader is kept. Fonts, sizes and colours are not: the reader’s own device decides how the
        text looks, which is what lets them make it larger or have it read aloud.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Pictures (figures)
      
      
      A picture in the text is cut out of the scan. Click a picture in the text to open its panel below the text:
      
      
        
        
      • Alt text describes the picture for someone who cannot see it: what it shows and why it matters
          here. Leave it empty only for a purely decorative picture. AI autofill asks the model to write a
          description for you to check.
        • 
        
      • Draw crop: drag a box over the picture on the scan. Drag the box to move it, or its corners to
          resize it.
        • 
        
      • ↺ ↻ turn the picture a quarter turn.
        • 
        
      • wrap text lets the text flow beside the picture; use the alignment buttons to put it on the left or
          right.
        • 
      
      
      
      To add a picture the model missed, put the cursor where it belongs, click +Fig, and draw its box on
        the scan.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Tables
      
      
      Sometimes the model runs a table together as ordinary text. To fix it, select that text, click
        +Table, and drag a box over the table on the scan. The model reads just that part of the page again and
        puts a proper table in place of the selected text. Check it, then save the page as usual.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Page number, skip and notes
      
      
      Below the text are settings for the page as a whole:
      
      
      
        
      p.
      
        
      The page number printed on the page (such as 42 or xii). This is the number readers see and
          cite in the finished files. Leave it empty for a page with no printed number.
      
        
      starts mid-¶ / ends mid-¶
      
        
      Tick these when a paragraph runs over from one page to the next, so it is joined back into one paragraph in the
          finished files.
      
        
      skip
      
        
      Leaves the page out of the finished files entirely: for blank pages, library stamps and the like.
      
        
      Notes
      
        
      The model writes here when it was unsure of something, and you can add notes for whoever checks the page next. A
          ⚠ on the Notes button means the page has notes. The Notes button shows and hides this box.
      
        
      Approve
      
        
      Saves the page and marks it checked (green). Approve & next does the same and moves on.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Saving your work
      
      
      Save (or Ctrl+S) saves every page you have changed; the number on the button says how many. A saved
        page that is not approved stays amber.
      
      
      If you move to another page without saving, your changes are kept and still marked with ✎, even if the program is
        closed, until you save them.
      
      
      Someone else, or Claude as an assistant, may be working on the same document. If a page you are editing is changed by
        them, a yellow bar appears above the text. You can load their version or keep your own; nothing is overwritten
        without asking.
      
      
      All your work is kept on this computer. To keep a copy of it, or carry on at another computer, use
        File > Export project.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Making the finished files
      
      
      Build puts all the pages together into one web page (HTML) and one e-book (EPUB). It saves any unsaved
        pages first. Its results appear at the bottom of the window, with a link to preview the web page and any warnings, such
        as a skipped heading level.
      
      
      Pages that are not approved are still included, so you can build at any time to see how it is going.
      
      
      Download the files with the HTML and EPUB buttons in the top bar. Unless you change it
        in Settings, the HTML file is one self-contained file that opens with a double-click and can be uploaded to a learning management system as it is.
      
      
      Validate checks the finished files against accessibility rules. Problems are listed as errors
        (should be fixed), warnings (worth a look) and notes. Click a page number in the list to go there. Build again after
        fixing.
      
      
      Properties… changes the title, author and language that the finished files carry.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      The File menu
      
      
      
        
      Open PDF…
      
        
      Start on a scan, or go back to one opened before. The PDF stays where it is. If you open the same PDF again, even
          from another folder or under another name, your earlier work comes back with it.
      
        
      Save
      
        
      Saves every changed page.
      
        
      Recent projects
      
        
      Every document you have worked on, the most recent first. A project is the work you have done on one PDF.
      
        
      Manage projects…
      
        
      Lists all your projects. Locate tells Unscanner where a PDF went after it was moved or renamed.
          Remove deletes the work done on a PDF (never the PDF itself); you are offered an export first.
      
        
      Export project
      
        
      Saves the work on this document as one file, to keep beside the PDF as a backup or to hand to a colleague.
      
        
      Import project…
      
        
      Loads an exported project file. It only works with the PDF it was made from; you can leave the PDF out if it has
          been opened on this computer before.
      
        
      Settings…
      
        
      See Settings.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Settings
      
      
      Most settings are set up once, often by whoever supports Unscanner at your institution.
      
      
      
        
      Backend and model
      
        
      Which AI model reads the pages: Claude, from Anthropic, or a model your institution runs itself. Each needs its
          address or key filled in.
      
        
      Parallel pages
      
        
      How many pages are read at the same time. Lower it if the model reports being busy or overloaded.
      
        
      When a model refuses a page
      
        
      A model sometimes declines to transcribe a page, mistaking it for a copyright problem. Here you choose a second model
          to try instead, and whether to send the document’s title along (a famous title can make refusals more likely).
      
        
      Edit prompts…
      
        
      The standing instructions the model is given with every page: who is doing this work and why, and your house style
          for headings, footnotes, tables and pictures.
      
        
      Export
      
        
      How the finished files look: book-style paragraphs, justified text, whether to show page numbers, pictures inside
          the HTML file, a list of headings at the top of the HTML, and smaller (grey or JPEG) pictures. Build again for a
          change to take effect.
      
      
      
      
      When you transcribe, the Transcribe… box also lets you choose which pages to read (for example
        all, 5 or 1-10,14), redo pages already read, and add instructions for just this
        run, such as “read the left column before the right”.

      Words used in Unscanner
      
      
      
        
      Alt text
      
        
      A written description of a picture, read aloud by a screen reader in its place.
      
        
      EPUB
      
        
      The standard e-book format, opened by e-book readers and apps such as Apple Books or Thorium.
      
        
      HTML
      
        
      A web page. It opens in any web browser.
      
        
      Project
      
        
      The work done on one PDF: its text, corrections and approvals.
      
        
      Screen reader
      
        
      Software that reads the screen aloud for people who are blind or have low vision.
      
        
      Transcribe
      
        
      Write out the text of a page word for word.
      
        
      WCAG
      
        
      The international accessibility standard the finished files are made to meet.
